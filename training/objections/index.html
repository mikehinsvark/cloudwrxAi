Objection handling

Welcome the question, answer briefly, and bring specific issues to the right person.

16 questions

This sounds too good to be true.+

That is fair. This is a possible licensing opportunity, not a promised payout. The provider can explain the process and written terms, and you decide whether it fits.

What happens to my data? What about privacy?+

Ask about exclusions, de-identification, human review, approved recipients, and use and retention limits. Removing identifiers does not make every dataset anonymous or risk-free. Involve your counsel and IT team.

Do you need access to our systems?+

Not for an introduction. Start with a description of systems and records. Do not send me files or credentials. If you proceed, authorized staff should agree with the provider on any later export or access.

Is this legal?+

That depends on rights, contracts, privacy obligations, professional rules, and the proposed use. Have your counsel review the scope and agreement. A referral partner cannot determine legal clearance.

Am I selling my data or losing ownership?+

The opportunity is described as licensing for an agreed use. Review ownership, exclusivity, permitted use, and any sublicensing rights in the actual terms before deciding.

Could this help a competitor?+

Ask who can receive or use records, whether that use could affect your business, and what exclusions or restrictions can be agreed. Do not assume anonymity eliminates competitive risk.

What does it cost?+

The playbook describes no upfront owner fee and a success-based model. Ask the provider to identify all fees, deductions, and conditions in writing before proceeding.

How much is it worth?+

It depends on the records, rights, quality, uniqueness, and buyer demand. A public range or example is not an offer for your company. The provider must review the opportunity.

How long does it take to get paid?+

The current provider page says usually 90–120 days after signing; the original brochure used about 60 days from introduction. Neither guarantees a date. Confirm the buyer’s process and payment terms.

Which AI labs are involved?+

I would not name a buyer for your company before a real proposal exists. Ask the provider to identify the proposed buyer and permitted use when you review an offer.

Our records are a mess. We are not technical.+

Start with a high-level list of tools and approximate years of history. The provider can explain what is useful and what work would be needed. You do not need to prepare files for me.

How much work is this for my team?+

The first discussion is intended to be short. A transaction can require substantial IT, rights, privacy, and legal review. Ask for a realistic scope and division of responsibilities before committing.

Why not go directly to an AI lab?+

You can evaluate different routes. A provider may help with preparation and buyer discussions. Compare services, fees, and terms rather than assuming one route guarantees a better result.

Can you just send information?+

Of course. I can send the overview or my client link. If you would like a follow-up, what time and channel work for you?

I need to talk with my partner, CFO, or lawyer.+

Absolutely. Invite them to the discussion so everyone hears the same answers. Include the people responsible for the business, records, and obligations.

I am not interested.+

Understood, and thanks for letting me know. I will close the loop. Leave the door open without pushing, and honor any request to stop contact.
