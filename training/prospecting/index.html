Start conversations

Prospecting plays

Start with people who already trust you, then build a repeatable prospecting rhythm.

Eight ways to find the next introduction.

Start with your own book

Past benefits, tax-credit, funding, and business-service clients may already know you. Lead with something specific to their business.

Watch for change

A merger, acquisition, closure, sale, or system migration can make a records conversation timely. Research the business before reaching out.

Meet centers of influence

CPAs, attorneys, brokers, fractional CFOs, bankers, and turnaround professionals know owners. Explore the partner track with them.

Talk with IT providers

MSPs and integrators understand the tools and retained history. Their own business may also warrant a fit review.

Search by technology

Use authorized prospecting tools to research companies, decision makers, shared systems, and digital history. Verify details before contact.

Use professional directories

Bar and CPA directories, agency lists, chambers, and business journals can reveal relevant firms. A listing is not permission or verified fit.

Join rooms full of owners

Offer a practical introduction at a chamber or trade group: “Could your operational records have another use?”

Publish something useful

Explain a workflow example or owner question. Invite a conversation without promising a payout.

A starting rhythm—not a quota.

10owner conversations started
5high-level qualifying chats
2fit reviews or introductions
1potential partner conversation

Suggested daily activity from the brochure. Adjust to your relationships, capacity, and actual results.

Your first week

DayOne useful focus
MondayList 50 warm-market businesses. Note approximate desk staff, systems, and who you know.
TuesdayCall your 10 strongest relationships. Agree on a next step with anyone interested.
WednesdayPersonalize first-touch emails for relevant contacts. Build context before sending.
ThursdayList 10 centers of influence and start partner conversations.
FridayFollow up appropriately, research additional firms, and review what worked.
Weekly reviewCount conversations, fit reviews, intro calls, and partner discussions—not just messages sent.
