Who to target

Look for digital history, useful operational context, and an owner who can authorize a review.

20+people working at desks
3+ yearsaccessible business records
5+ toolsbrochure prospecting guideline
US / CAcurrent provider geography

The provider currently lists US or Canada, 3+ years, and 20+ desk staff. The brochure also suggests 5+ software systems and English-language records. These are screening guides; the provider decides actual fit.

Three questions to start the conversation.

  1. “About how many people work at a desk, on shared email and software?”

    Focus on people whose work creates shared digital records, rather than total field headcount.

  2. “What software runs the business, and how long have you used it?”

    Ask about email, chat, CRM, tickets, project tools, and the history they still retain.

  3. “Who would decide on licensing company records?”

    Bring that person—and relevant IT or legal colleagues—into the discovery conversation.

Where to look first

Software & IT

SaaS, MSPs, development agencies, cybersecurity, analytics, and cloud consultancies.

Finance & accounting

CPA, bookkeeping, tax advisory, fractional CFO, payroll, valuation, and back-office firms.

Legal

Business law, estate planning, litigation support, and other practices with separable operational workflows.

Operations & logistics

Freight, 3PL, distribution, warehousing, procurement, manufacturing support, and field service operations.

Energy & industrial

Engineering, equipment service, energy logistics, utility support, maintenance, and safety consultancies.

Service & back office

Consulting, BPOs, staffing, property management, agencies, associations, and administrative operations.

Smaller specialist firms

The brochure notes possible exceptions for law and accounting firms. Ask the provider about current exceptions; do not present an internal headcount example as approval. Rights and confidentiality review still apply.

Reasons to explore

  • Years of cloud-based records.
  • Regular help-desk, CRM, or project activity.
  • Documented decisions, handoffs, and approvals.
  • An authorized decision maker willing to review.
  • An upcoming sale, merger, closure, or system migration.

Reasons to pause

  • Little retained digital history.
  • Unclear ownership or permission.
  • Mostly field crews with few shared systems.
  • Privileged, medical, minors’, classified, or export-controlled material.
  • No clear way to separate restricted information.
Ask about categories, not contents

Examples include email, Slack or Teams, Jira or Zendesk, CRM, SOPs, code history, and consented recordings. Do not request samples yourself. The privacy lesson explains what to exclude.
